<section class="work-section work-feature">
  <div>
    <h2>A deployment vehicle carrying a complete observing system.</h2>
    <p>DPI-3—often shortened to “DPI” and nicknamed “Big Friend”—is a Bellamare-built, actively controlled undulating towed vehicle used by the Northern Gulf of Alaska LTER program. It carries three ISIIS-DPI plankton cameras together with acoustic and environmental instruments. The cameras produce high-resolution line-scan shadowgraph imagery, while DPI-3 provides the controlled deployment platform and carries the broader observing payload.</p>
    <p>Organisms and particles are observed in place, without collection or physical handling, as DPI-3 crosses depth layers and hydrographic gradients.</p>
    <p>Each image is part of a spatially and temporally indexed observation stream. Time, position, vehicle depth, camera, sampling context, environmental measurements, and processing history all matter alongside the pixels.</p>

<section class="work-section">
  <h2>A data stream measured in millions of liters and hundreds of terabytes.</h2>
  <p>The reported UAF/NGA operating configuration creates an unusually demanding image-processing problem. Exact rates vary with camera and acquisition settings, but the scale is constant: sustained shipboard acquisition, sparse targets within an immense background, and far more imagery than a science team can inspect manually.</p>

<section class="work-section">
  <h2>Fine-scale imagery changes what scientists can see.</h2>
  <p>Traditional plankton nets remain essential, but they can miss fine spatial structure and damage fragile gelatinous organisms. Imagery from the ISIIS-DPI plankton cameras preserves organisms in their oceanographic setting. Research from the 2022–2023 DPI-3 deployments found aggregations of ctenophores, hydromedusae, and siphonophores around frontal features, reported previously undetected species records, and showed that gelatinous zooplankton were more prominent in the ecosystem than historical sampling had indicated.</p>
  <div class="work-card-grid">
    <div class="pelagia-card"><h3>Biological distributions</h3><p>Map abundance, size, and fragile gelatinous zooplankton across depth and distance.</p></div>
    <div class="pelagia-card"><h3>Environmental context</h3><p>Relate biological patches to temperature, salinity, oxygen, acoustics, and frontal structure.</p></div>
    <div class="pelagia-card"><h3>Defensible records</h3><p>Create quality-controlled, georeferenced observations for ecological analysis and model training.</p></div>
