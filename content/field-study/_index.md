<section class="field-studies-hero">
  <p class="pelagia-kicker">From deployment to defensible data</p>
  <h2 class="work-hero-title">Imaging workflows built for the field.</h2>
  <p class="lead">Pelagia supports pelagic imaging across the full range of research operations—from real-time interpretation during acquisition to processing accumulated collections after a campaign. It is designed to scale from focused laboratory and pilot datasets to continuous, multi-camera surveys producing hundreds of terabytes.</p>
  <p class="field-studies-intro">Pelagia’s development is grounded in shadowgraph imagery produced by ISIIS-DPI plankton cameras, but its workflow is not defined by a single instrument. By organizing imagery together with acquisition context, environmental observations, processing history, and scientific review, Pelagia can be adapted to different imaging platforms, deployment strategies, and research questions.</p>
  <div class="field-study-spectrum" aria-label="Pelagia field-workflow range">
    <div><strong>Live → archived</strong><span>Interpret observations during acquisition or revisit existing collections.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Focused → continuous</strong><span>Work with a reference library, pilot dataset, or sustained survey.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Instrument-aware</strong><span>Adapt the workflow while keeping platform-specific context explicit.</span></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="work-section">
  <p class="pelagia-kicker">One workflow, different scales</p>
  <h2>Meet each program where it operates.</h2>
  <p>Field imaging programs differ in duration, acquisition rate, platform, staffing, and scientific goals. Pelagia provides a common path from source imagery to reviewable observations without requiring every project to operate at the same scale.</p>
  <div class="work-principles field-use-grid">
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>Real-time operations</h3>
      <p>Monitor incoming imagery, processing status, and biological observations while sampling is underway.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>Focused collections</h3>
      <p>Work with benchtop imagery, reference libraries, pilot deployments, and targeted experiments without survey-scale infrastructure.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>Sustained surveys</h3>
      <p>Process continuous or repeated observations from towed vehicles, profilers, moorings, and other high-volume platforms.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>Accumulated archives</h3>
      <p>Apply transparent, repeatable processing and review to collections acquired before Pelagia was deployed.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="work-section work-feature field-platforms">
  <div>
    <p class="pelagia-kicker">Platform adaptable</p>
    <h2>The instrument changes. The scientific workflow remains recognizable.</h2>
    <p>Pelagia’s strongest demonstrated fit is high-volume shadowgraph imagery from ISIIS-DPI plankton cameras, but its underlying workflow extends beyond that instrument family. Towed line-scan systems, area-scan cameras, profilers, stationary observatories, benchtop imagers, and other platforms all produce variations of the same operational problem.</p>
    <p>Preserve the source observation. Connect it to time, position, instrument state, and environmental context. Identify useful targets. Review results. Create defensible data products. Pelagia provides a common environment for that work while allowing instrument-specific acquisition details and processing methods to remain explicit.</p>

<section class="work-section">
  <p class="pelagia-kicker">Before, during, and after</p>
  <h2>Designed to facilitate fieldwork—not only post-process it.</h2>
  <div class="work-card-grid field-stage-grid">
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>Before deployment</h3>
      <p>Define imagery sources, metadata, processing methods, labels, review needs, and expected outputs before acquisition begins.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>During acquisition</h3>
      <p>Monitor image streams and processing, identify operational problems, and begin biological interpretation while sampling continues.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="pelagia-card">
      <h3>After recovery</h3>
      <p>Resume processing, conduct scientific review, refine methods, and export traceable products for analysis and publication.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
